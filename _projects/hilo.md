Wireless transmitters that run on microwatts produce weak signals, easily lost to interference and fading at particular frequencies. Sending the same data on two frequency bands at once adds redundancy without sacrificing bitrate or latency, but conventional multi-band radios need duplicated radio chains or power-hungry synthesizers.

HILO obtains both bands from a single tunnel-diode oscillator, a microwatt-scale signal source. The harmonics inherently generated by the nonlinear tunnel diode, which earlier oscillator designs suppress, serve as a redundant channel for reliable transmission across two bands. A 433 MHz tone from an external emitter injection-locks the oscillator, fixing its frequency and so its second harmonic at 868 MHz. The same circuit transmits on both bands and downconverts incoming signals in both, the first step of reception, consuming under 210 µW.

HILO was presented at the IEEE International Conference on RFID 2026.
